# reel-hooks

The hook is the first 3 seconds: what the viewer hears, sees and reads before deciding to stay.
Most reels are lost there, not in the middle.

## Before you write

Read `reel-agent/profile.md` and `reel-agent/lessons.md` if they exist. Get the topic and the one
thing the viewer gets from the reel (the payoff). If the payoff is unclear, ask: a hook can't
promise what the body doesn't deliver.

## Write

1. Pick 4-6 formulas from `references/hook-formulas.md` that fit the topic.
2. Write 8-10 candidate lines in the creator's dialect. Keep each under ~10 spoken words; four
   is often best.
3. For each, write the three channels in one row:
   - **Say:** the spoken line.
   - **Show:** frame 0. The creator's face plus one on-screen element (a crossed-out logo, a
     number, the result). It must make sense with the sound off.
   - **Read:** the caption text on screen, at most ~6 words.

## Score each line (out of 10)

| Test | Points |
|---|---|
| **Stake:** names something the viewer already has or wants (their account, job, money, time), not just a brand or a tool | 3 |
| **Payoff or stakes inside 3 s** | 2 |
| **Specific:** a number, a name, a result | 2 |
| **True,** and the body pays it off | 2 |
| **Short and sayable** in one breath in the dialect | 1 |

Lines that fail "true" score 0, however good they sound.

## Output

Write `reel-agent/reels/<slug>/hook.md`: the table of candidates with scores, your top pick and a
runner-up of a **different** formula type. Tell the creator to film both as separate takes (each
alone, 2-3 takes) and keep the one that holds better.

## What the data behind these rules says

From the reels of @alihaidar.dev, who built this team (Instagram Insights):

- An opening on a **shared stake** («شركات البرمجة», software companies) had a 44% skip rate; an
  opening on a **brand name** (an AI video tool) had 53%.
- A **faceless** opening (UI on a phone, AI voice clone) had an **85.7%** skip rate and a 3-second
  average watch. The breakout reels opened on his face making a bold claim.

These are one creator's numbers, not laws. Check them against your own in `lessons.md`.
